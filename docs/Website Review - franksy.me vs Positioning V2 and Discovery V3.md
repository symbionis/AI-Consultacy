# Website Review: franksy.me

**Reviewed against:** Positioning V2 and Discovery Conversation V3
**Date:** 19 May 2026

---

## Summary

The website is strongly aligned with Positioning V2 in most areas. The hero copy, three-part offer structure, funnel design, audience targeting, vocabulary, and tone all reflect the positioning document faithfully. One significant divergence stands out: the 5 Levels framework is displayed prominently on the homepage, which the positioning document explicitly says should not happen. A few smaller tensions are worth addressing.

## 1. Hero & Messaging

### What the positioning says

The hero should have three layers: H1 naming what and how it's different, a sub-head naming audience and shape concretely, and a tagline making the promise in plain words. The recommended copy is: "AI coaching, in your voice" / "For entrepreneurs and small teams. Six weeks of working sessions. Tools that fit you." / "Less weight. More room. A second mind to think with."

### What the website does

The H1, sub-head, and tagline match the positioning document precisely. The only difference: the website sub-head reads "For entrepreneurs and small teams" without the second line ("Six weeks of working sessions. Tools that fit you."). This makes the hero cleaner but removes the concrete shape (six weeks, tools).

### Recommendation

Consider whether the missing second sub-head line is a deliberate simplification or an oversight. The six-weeks framing sets expectations early and could reduce friction on the connect call. If omitted intentionally for visual economy, that's a reasonable trade-off — but worth testing.

## 2. Three-Part Offer

### What the positioning says

Three layered things you sell: Understanding (a humanistic diagnostic), Companionship (six sessions walking alongside the transition), and A system they keep (Cowork install, Skills, routines, Project structure that works after you stop). The closing line: "structural, not aspirational."

### What the website does

The three cards (01 Understanding, 02 Continuity, 03 A system you keep) map closely. Card 2 is labelled "Continuity" on the website vs. "Companionship" in the positioning. Both capture the same idea but with different emotional registers — continuity is more operational, companionship is more relational. The closing callout quote "Structural, not aspirational" appears on the website, matching the positioning.

### Recommendation

The naming divergence on card 2 is worth a conscious decision. "Companionship" is the stronger word for this audience — entrepreneurs rarely get someone walking alongside them, and naming that directly is part of the positioning's emotional edge. "Continuity" is safer but flatter. If the concern was that "companionship" sounds too therapeutic for a public page, that's worth testing — but the positioning doc built the argument specifically around the word.

Card 3 says "your own workflows, your own routines, custom skills that work alongside you" — the positioning is more specific: "Your Cowork install, your Skills, your routines, your Project structure." The website correctly strips the product names (Cowork, Skills, Project) for a public audience, which aligns with the vocabulary guidance to keep jargon internal.

## 3. Funnel & Pricing

### What the positioning says

Three steps: a free 15-minute connect call (qualifier), a 60-minute Discovery session (CHF 250 reference rate, preferential for first 15 entrepreneurs through June 2026), and Coaching Together (six weekly sessions, CHF 1,200–1,800, accepting a small first-cohort through summer 2026).

### What the website does

The three steps match exactly. The launch scarcity signals are present: "preferential rate for the first 15 entrepreneurs" on Discovery, "currently accepting a small number of first-cohort clients through summer 2026" on Coaching Together. However, no prices appear on the website — not even the CHF 250 reference rate.

### Recommendation

If pricing is intentionally absent to qualify through conversation, that's a valid strategy — but the positioning doc names the figures as part of the positioning, not just internal. The absence could increase connect-call volume from people who can't afford the engagement, or it could feel opaque to the audience you're targeting (operators who appreciate transparency). Worth deciding explicitly: is this a "price on request" positioning, or should at least the Discovery rate appear?

## 4. The 5 Levels Framework — Key Divergence

### What the positioning says

Explicit and emphatic: "The L1–L5 framework is revealed inside paid sessions. It does not appear on the website, in posts, or in cold outreach. It's the diagnostic instrument that makes the discovery session feel earned — putting it on the homepage trades the most valuable conversational asset for a piece of marketing copy that doesn't convert anyway. Hold the line."

### What the website does

The entire 5-level framework is displayed on the homepage in a dedicated section, with all five levels named, described, and visually styled. L3 is highlighted as "the shift." There is a link to a full framework page (/framework/). This is a direct contradiction of the positioning guidance.

### Why this matters

The positioning argument is that the framework is the discovery session's most valuable moment — the point where someone sees where they stand and the gap becomes concrete. If they've already read the five levels on the website, that moment is pre-spent. The framework becomes informational instead of diagnostic. The discovery session loses its centrepiece.

### Recommendation

This is the single most consequential divergence in the review. Either the positioning guidance was right and the framework should come off the homepage, or the decision to put it on the homepage was tested and found to convert — in which case the positioning document should be updated. Both can't be true at once. If the framework stays, the Discovery Conversation V3 script (which introduces the framework at minute 30 as a reveal) needs to be reworked for people who've already seen it.

## 5. Discovery Session Description vs. V3 Script

### What the website promises

The website describes the Discovery session deliverables as:

- Where you sit on the 5 Levels of AI Fluency
- What your next step in capability could be
- Any questions you've been wanting to ask but haven't had the right person for
- A written document 24 hours later, two experiments to try this week

### What the V3 script actually covers

The V3 conversation script is structured in four phases:

- Phase 1 (12 min): Their week concretely — what they did, what took time, what only moves when they do it
- Phase 2 (13 min): Recurring patterns and waiting projects
- Phase 3 (8 min): Their relationship with tools
- Phase 4 (12 min): The framework reveal, and what's next

### The tension

The website's third promise — "any questions you've been wanting to ask but haven't had the right person for" — doesn't appear in the V3 script at all. There's no phase for open Q&A. The V3 script is tightly structured around diagnosis, not Q&A. This could create a mismatch where someone arrives expecting to ask about tokens, subscriptions, and pricing plans — exactly the questions V3 says to gently set aside.

The V3 script also opens with a strong diagnostic stance: "Start from the factual: what they did last week, what takes time, what recurs." The website describes this as "an hour to understand your world, your needs, and where there's weight to lift" — which is warmer but less specific. Not necessarily wrong, but the V3 script's emphasis on concreteness (last week, real tasks, no generalities) is the positioning's differentiator, and the website softens it.

### Recommendation

Either add a brief open-questions window to the V3 script (even 5 minutes at the end), or remove that promise from the website. Also consider whether the website description of the Discovery could lean more into the concrete, factual tone that makes V3 distinctive — "We start from your actual week" rather than the softer "understand your world."

## 6. Vocabulary & Tone

### Words to avoid (from positioning)

Productivity, automation, AI-native, 10x, unlock, transform, empower, leverage (as verb), humanistic/agentic (public-facing), system/operation (in hero context).

### Website compliance

Clean. None of the banned words appear on the homepage. The site uses the preferred vocabulary consistently:

- "Weight" / "carrying" / "room" appear throughout
- "Waiting" used naturally ("what's been waiting")
- "Voice" in the H1 and card 3
- "Humanistic" and "agentic" correctly absent from public copy

### One note

Card 1 uses "A humanistic diagnostic" as its sub-heading. The positioning says to keep "humanistic" internal. In practice, this is borderline — it appears as a card heading, not in the hero or in posts. But it's worth flagging: the positioning was specific about this word staying out of public-facing copy.

## 7. Additional Observations

**The blockquote voice.** The website uses two blockquote callouts that read as Frank speaking directly. This is effective and consistent with the positioning's emphasis on personal voice. The quote about understanding rhythms and habits aligns with the "what you're not" section of the positioning: you're not a tools trainer, you're walking someone through a transition.

**The "univers" typo.** Card 1 reads "your univers" — likely a French spelling that slipped through. Should be "universe" or reworked entirely ("your world" might fit the tone better).

**About section.** The about teaser is strong and aligns with the positioning's entrepreneur-specific edge: the 1998 internet parallel, the pattern of technology arriving and people moving from fear to adoption. This is the kind of concrete story the positioning calls for.

**V3 script's post-session capture.** The V3 script includes a private post-session capture section (level that resonated, one concrete use case, a project they're carrying, price sensitivity, referral potential). None of this is mentioned on the website, which is correct — it's operational. But it's worth noting that the V3 script is also a sales instrument: the referral question and price-sensitivity check show the conversation is doing commercial qualification alongside diagnosis. The two documents should stay in sync if the discovery format evolves.
